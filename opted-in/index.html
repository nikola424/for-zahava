D1: 3 Proven Steps To Open Every Call

Preview The first minute decides the call.

The first minute decides the call.

Hey {FIRST NAME},

Most calls are lost in the first minute, long before the money part.

Here’s how I open every call, in 3 steps:

• Ask why they’re here
• Repeat it back
• Tell them where the call is going

1. Ask why they’re here.

“Do you know why we’re on the phone today?”

Now they tell you why they need you, in their own words. That’s worth more than any pitch you could give.

2. Repeat it back.

“So what I’m hearing is you want lower costs and to keep your doctor. Did I get that right?”

When they say yes, they feel heard. And you have the reason they’ll buy.

3. Tell them where the call is going.

“Here’s what we’ll do. I’ll ask a few questions, show you your options, and if one fits, we’ll get it handled today.”

That last part matters. You just told them, calmly, that this call ends with a decision.

So, one more time: ask why they’re here, repeat it back, and tell them where the call is going.

D3: The Exact Words For The Money Part

Preview Your voice changes. Here’s why.

Your voice changes. Here’s why.

Hey {FIRST NAME},

Something happens to most of us when we get to the price.

Our voice goes higher. We speed up. We say “but no pressure at all”. The client hears all of it.

Here’s how I walk through the money part, step by step:

1. Slow down before you say the number. Take one breath. Calm voice, normal speed.

2. Say it once, and stop. No “but”. No apology after it.

3. Tie it to what they told you. “And that keeps your doctor, like you wanted.”

4. Use the good news and bad news line when there’s a catch. “I’ve got good news and bad news.” People lean in every time.

5. Ask for the decision. “Would it be the end of the world if you had this taken care of today?”

6. Be quiet. Let them answer. The first person to fill the silence usually gives something away.

Try steps 1 and 2 on your next call. Just those two. Your voice will tell you the difference.

D7: The 3 Objections That Cost You The Most Sales

Preview And the words I use for each one.

And the words I use for each one.

Hey {FIRST NAME},

Objections aren’t rejection. They’re redirection. The client is telling you what they need to hear before they say yes.

Here are the three I hear most, and what I say to each.

1. “I need to think about it.”

This one costs you the most, because it sounds polite and it ends the call. Don’t agree and hang up. Get specific:

“Totally fair. What part do you want to think about?”

Then answer that one part. When it’s handled, ask: “Would it be the end of the world if you had this taken care of today?”

2. “I need to talk to my spouse.”

Respect it. Then make it easy:

“Of course. Is your spouse home now? I’m happy to walk you both through it in five minutes.”

A lot of the time, they are. And if not, you book the time together before you hang up.

3. “I’m happy with what I have.”

Don’t argue. Agree, and get curious:

“I love that. What do you like most about it?”

Their answer tells you what matters to them. Often it’s something your plan does better, and now they said it, not you.
