05

Mechanism

Word tracks, not winging it

Most of us were taught that being friendly and patient makes a client trust us. On the phone it works the other way around. When you sound unsure, the client feels it, and they get off the call. What they trust is a calm voice that knows where the call is going.

So I don’t wing my calls. I use word tracks, and they cover three parts of every call.

Part 1

The opener

This changes the first minute. Most reps open with small talk and lose control of the call. I ask, “Do you know why we’re on the phone today?” Now the client tells you why they need you, in their own words, and you lead the call from there.

Part 2

The money part

This changes what happens to your voice when you talk about price. I use three tones: Captain, Caring and Cheerleader. Captain when you need to lead, Caring when they’re worried, and Cheerleader when they’re ready. When you know which tone to use, your voice stops changing.

Part 3

The close

This changes what happens when they push back. Objections aren’t rejection. They’re redirection. I use my AVR Framework to answer the objection, and then one calm question, like “Would it be the end of the world if you had this taken care of today?” Most clients answer it themselves, and you never had to push.

That’s it. Three parts of the call, and the right words for each one.
